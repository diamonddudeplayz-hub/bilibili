## The floor mechanics — why bounces cluster at ~2,800–3,000 (added 24 Nov 2026)

**It's all the multiple.** Peak Feb 2021 ~10,945 → now ~3,467 (−68%), *while* constituent earnings (Tencent, Alibaba, Meituan, Xiaomi, NetEase…) **grew** over the five years. Price down + earnings up ⇒ the entire de-rate is **multiple compression** — fwd P/E from high-20s/~30x at the peak to **low-teens now.** None of the pain is fundamentals.
- **Bull math:** a re-rate applies a *normalized multiple to a bigger earnings base* → the index can *exceed* the old high; levered single-names (BILI) far more. The +50/70/100% isn't hope, it's arithmetic — *if* the compression is a cyclical overshoot.
- **Bear counter:** the multiple fell for a *reason* (jurisdiction / geopolitical tail — today's US chip curbs are that risk re-pricing live). If permanent, no snap-back; you collect the **earnings yield via buybacks** instead (still fine at low-teens P/E + 4–6% buyback yields, just not the double). **The whole debate = cyclical overshoot vs. permanent re-pricing.**

**Why the floor clusters at a similar *index level* even though catalysts are untimeable/exogenous:** the level isn't causal — it's where three things coincide:
1. **A valuation floor** (a trough multiple below which the market won't go absent insolvency). Earnings were roughly stable-to-up, so "trough multiple × similar earnings base" maps to a *similar index level.*
2. **The state-stability put**, which activates on *disorderly* selling = capitulation = *at* the floor.
3. **A Schelling point / trader memory** — everyone remembers 2,802 (Oct '22) and 3,006 (Jan '24) held; they buy there, and the state knows those are the lines where confidence breaks, so it defends around there. Self-fulfilling, not targeted.

**The edge that compounds:** because earnings grew while the level stayed ~flat, **each retest of ~3,000 is at a *cheaper multiple* than the last** — same price, more earnings underneath, lower P/E, better risk/reward. 3,000 in 2026 ≠ 3,000 in 2022; it's materially cheaper. The asymmetry *improves* every retest.

**Does an HSTECH crash hurt the Chinese consumer? Barely.** Household wealth is ~60–70% **property**, low-single-digit % equities; HSTECH is foreign/institutional/southbound-held HK-listed tech, not the mainland saver's balance sheet. So an index crash is **not a wealth-effect hit to consumption** (the *property* bust is the real one). It damages **confidence/signaling, capital formation (IPOs), foreign portfolios, and "the financial heft underpinning the tech push."** → This is *why* Beijing intervenes for **financial stability**, not as a consumption lever: the index isn't wired to the consumer.
